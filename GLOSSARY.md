# Commercial finance terms

## Capital structure

The combination of debt, equity and other funding used to finance an asset, project or business. A capital structure is assessed as a whole: the cheapest individual facility is not necessarily the strongest overall structure.

## Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

A measure of the cash available to meet scheduled principal and interest payments. A common expression is:

`cash available for debt service ÷ total debt service`

A result above 1.0 indicates that the measured cash flow exceeds the measured debt service. The required level and the permitted adjustments vary by lender and transaction.

## Interest cover ratio (ICR)

A measure of the capacity of income or earnings to meet interest expense. A common expression is:

`net operating income or earnings ÷ interest expense`

The numerator may differ between property and corporate transactions, so the lender's definition matters.

## Loan-to-value ratio (LVR)

The loan amount expressed as a percentage of the lender-accepted value of the security:

`loan amount ÷ accepted property value × 100`

The accepted value may not equal the purchase price, development cost or borrower's estimate.

## Loan-to-cost ratio (LTC)

The debt facility expressed as a percentage of the lender-accepted project cost:

`eligible debt ÷ accepted project cost × 100`

Lenders may exclude some costs, related-party amounts, contingencies or previously incurred expenditure.

## Gross realisation value (GRV)

The estimated aggregate value of completed project stock, generally before selling costs, tax and debt repayment. Development lenders may use GRV when testing leverage, feasibility and exit risk.

## Gross floor area (GFA)

The total floor area of a building measured under the relevant planning or valuation convention. It is not interchangeable with net lettable area or gross lettable area.

## Gross site area (GSA)

The total area of a site before any lender, valuer or planning adjustment. Where only part of a site is developable or usable, gross site area can overstate the effective land area.

## Senior debt

Debt that ranks ahead of subordinated or mezzanine capital in the agreed repayment and security order. Senior ranking does not remove project, valuation, covenant or enforcement risk.

## Mezzanine finance

Subordinated funding that sits between senior debt and sponsor equity. It usually carries a higher cost than senior debt because of its lower ranking and higher risk. Its terms may include interest, fees, profit participation or other returns.

## Preferred equity

Equity capital with contractual priority over ordinary equity for distributions or repayment. Despite its negotiated priority, it remains structurally different from senior secured debt.

## Vendor finance

Funding provided by a seller to support the buyer's acquisition. It may take the form of deferred consideration, a loan or another contractual payment arrangement. Its ranking and treatment by the senior lender must be agreed.

## Working capital

The short-term funding required to support day-to-day operations, including the timing gap between paying suppliers and collecting customer receipts. Facilities may include overdrafts, trade finance, invoice finance or revolving credit.

## Personal guarantee

A contractual promise by an individual to meet specified obligations of a borrower if the borrower does not. Scope, caps, supporting security and release conditions depend on the guarantee document.

## General security agreement (GSA)

An agreement granting a security interest over specified personal property of a grantor, often including most present and after-acquired assets of a company. Registration and priority are governed by the Personal Property Securities framework.

## Presales

Contracts to sell project stock before completion. A development lender may test presales for buyer concentration, deposit, sunset, valuation, finance and settlement risk before treating them as qualifying presales.

## Quantity surveyor (QS) report

An independent report used in construction lending to review costs, contracts, contingencies, progress and remaining cost to complete. The lender may require an initial report and progressive drawdown reports.

## Cost to complete

The lender-accepted amount required to finish a project from a stated date. A lender will commonly compare remaining committed funding and equity with the assessed cost to complete before approving further drawdowns.

## Exit strategy

The credible source and timing of repayment for a facility. Examples include asset sale, unit settlements, refinance from stabilised income or repayment from business cash flow. An intention to refinance is not, by itself, evidence that refinancing will be available.

## Capitalised interest

Interest added to the outstanding loan balance rather than paid periodically in cash. This can preserve near-term liquidity but increases the balance and total funding cost. Availability is subject to facility limits and lender policy.

## Covenant

A continuing obligation in a finance agreement, such as maintaining a minimum ICR, maximum LVR, reporting timetable or restriction on further debt. A breach may trigger review, remediation, default rights or repricing depending on the documents.

## Conditions precedent

Documents, approvals and actions that must be completed before a facility becomes available or a drawdown can occur. These can include executed security, valuations, insurance, equity evidence, approvals and satisfactory due diligence.
